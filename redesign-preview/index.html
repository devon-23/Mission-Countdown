03

Transcripts

Three kinds, because the real ones look nothing alike. A head of state's remarks come from a press office in Times New Roman. A control-room loop is typed, fixed-width, with elapsed-time stamps and call signs. A tribunal is line-numbered so lawyers can cite "page 41, line 12". No side panels, no stat tiles, no stamps. Context goes in a short note above or below the sheet.

THE WHITE HOUSE
Office of the Press Secretary
For Immediate ReleaseT−5.8 yr

ADDRESS BY THE PRESIDENT
TO THE NATION
ON THE PETROVA LINE

Oval Office

8:42 P.M. EST

THE PRESIDENT: My fellow citizens. Tonight the international scientific community has confirmed the existence of a previously unknown phenomenon in our solar system. It has been named the Petrova Line.

It is an arc of infrared light extending from the Sun to Venus. It has been verified independently by observatories on every inhabited continent. I want to be direct with you about what we know and about what we do not.

There is no immediate danger to the public. No action is required of you tonight.

However, data collected by the Japanese space agency, using its Amaterasu solar probe, has revealed something that concerns us deeply. The Sun's energy output is decreasing. At present that decline measures one one-hundredth of one percent. I am aware that this sounds like a small number. I am told it is not.

The rate of decline is accelerating. And the Petrova Line is growing brighter at precisely the rate that our Sun is growing dimmer.

*  *  *

Thank you. Good night.

END8:48 P.M. EST

04

Scientific findings

Set like a journal article: running head, title, authors with numbered affiliations, a bold abstract, two justified columns, a real plotted figure, a three-rule table, received / accepted dates. The typeface is STIX Two, the one scientific publishers actually use. Findings are stated the way a paper states them, with numbers and method, in place of "CONFIRMED BIOLOGICAL" tiles.

Petrova Task Force Technical ReportsVol. 1, No. 3  |  Biology
Research Article

Astrophage: a single-celled organism that absorbs stellar radiation and emits at 25.984 µm

R. Grace1*, Petrova Task Force Biology Division2
1Unaffiliated; work performed under Task Force single-investigator protocol. 2Petrova Task Force, Geneva. *Corresponding author.
Material returned from the Petrova line by the ArcLight probe is not dust or plasma. It is alive. We report opaque, motile cells about 10 µm across that hold a constant internal temperature of 96.415 °C, absorb every wavelength we could direct at them, and release stored energy as infrared light at a single wavelength, 25.984 µm, which they use for propulsion. The emission matches the Petrova line exactly. We propose the name Astrophage.

Samples and handling

ArcLight returned 174 particles from Venus orbit. All were transferred under argon to a single sealed laboratory. Because the hazard was unknown, one investigator handled the material for the first eleven days.

Optical microscopy at every available magnification showed featureless black dots (Fig. S1). Cells did not respond to visible or ultraviolet illumination and could not be imaged by transmission at any wavelength tested.

Emission

Individual cells emit brief pulses of infrared light. Across 2,140 recorded pulses the wavelength did not vary within instrument resolution (Fig. 1). Each pulse displaces the cell in the direction opposite to the emission. The organism is using light as thrust.

Temperature

Cells hold 96.415 °C regardless of surroundings. Placed against a 4 °C plate they did not cool. Placed in a furnace they absorbed the excess and remained at 96.415 °C.

News articles

A newspaper page: masthead, section line, headline, a standfirst that tells you why to read on, byline, dateline, a photograph with a caption and credit, one pull quote, one fact box. Written as reporting (who said what, who objected) so it reads differently from the official documents around it.

U.N. hands one administrator command of the Petrova response

Eva Stratt, a former European Space Agency official, can now direct any laboratory, launch site or budget on Earth. The vote was unanimous. The limits on her authority were left unwritten.

By Amara Okafor and Lukas Brandt
Reporting from New York and Geneva

NEW YORK — The General Assembly took eleven minutes on Tuesday to do something it has never done: place the scientific resources of every member state under the direction of a single person.

That person is Eva Stratt, a Dutch administrator best known inside the space industry for finishing other people's delayed projects. She was confirmed as director of the new Petrova Task Force without a dissenting vote and, delegates said afterwards, without much discussion of what she would be permitted to do.

The answer, according to the text of the resolution, is nearly anything. The director may requisition transport, manufacturing and personnel, override national regulators on matters connected to the Petrova Line, and spend without a ceiling.

"The directorship requires an administrator willing to be disliked. The board is satisfied that the nominee meets this requirement."Minute of the selection board, read into the record

Two delegations asked in committee when the powers would expire. The question was struck from the draft both times. A spokesman for the Secretary-General said only that the arrangement would last "for the duration of the emergency."

What the resolution grants

  • Diplomatic immunity in every cooperating nation
  • Priority on civil and military transport networks
  • Direction of all Petrova-related research
  • An uncapped budget, with no stated end date

Sun-dimming projections

Modelled on a climate assessment "Summary for Policymakers": numbered headline statements with a stated confidence level, then one real chart with a median line and an uncertainty range. The curve is fitted through the three numbers the novel gives (0.01% at disclosure, 1% after nine years, 5% after twenty), so every luminosity figure on the site can be read off the same line.
